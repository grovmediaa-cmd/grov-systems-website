WHAT A CUSTOMER EXPERIENCES · WHAT A STRATEGIST SEES

Three opportunities worth exploring

01 · VALUE

The outcome may be less tangible than the service itself.

From the customer's perspectiveI can see what the business does, but I may still be unsure what meaningfully changes for someone like me.

From the strategist's perspectiveThe opening message should connect the service to a specific customer problem and a credible desired outcome.

Example improvement
“A clearer path from [the challenge your customer faces] to [the outcome they want]—with a plan built around your needs.”

The system behind it: Align the homepage, offer pages and follow-up messaging around one consistent value proposition.

02 · TRUST

Proof could do more than reassure—it could help the buyer recognise themselves.

From the customer's perspectivePositive feedback is encouraging, but I may not know whether the experience is relevant to my situation.

From the strategist's perspectiveProof should show context: who the customer was, what they needed and what changed.

Example improvement
“Before working with us, [customer type] was dealing with [challenge]. Here's what they found most valuable about the experience…”

The system behind it: Build a repeatable process to collect, organise and place relevant customer proof across decision points.

03 · ACTION

The next step may ask for commitment before explaining the experience.

From the customer's perspectiveI'm interested, but I don't know what happens after I submit the form or make contact.

From the strategist's perspectiveSet expectations beside the CTA and make the first step feel clear, proportionate and low-friction.

Example improvement
“Once you send your enquiry, we'll review what you need and get back to you within [timeframe] with the most suitable next step.”

The system behind it: Connect enquiry capture, acknowledgement, routing and follow-up so the promised experience is consistently delivered.

A moment the customer may pause

After deciding the offer could be relevant, a visitor may hesitate if the next step feels uncertain. This is a hypothesis to validate—not observed visitor behaviour.
